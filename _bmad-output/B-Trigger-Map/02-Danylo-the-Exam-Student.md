# Danylo the Exam Student - Primary Persona

> Primary target - the deadline-driven learner who turns exam urgency into a focused paid trial

**Priority:** PRIMARY  
**Role in Flywheel:** Creates the sharpest first-launch booking path through exam-specific trust and urgency  
**Created:** 2026-06-25  

---

## Profile Summary

Danylo is preparing for a high-stakes English exam: NMT, EVI, Cambridge, TOEFL, CELPIP, or a similar goal. He is not casually browsing schools. He has a deadline, a score target, and a growing sense that scattered self-study is not enough.

He matters because his decision can happen quickly when the page gives him the right proof: exact exam coverage, teacher expertise, lesson structure, and a trial that feels diagnostic rather than generic.

## Background

### Education & Learning Context

**Current situation:** Danylo is preparing for an exam that affects admission, graduation, work, or an international plan.

**Learning journey:** He has likely used school lessons, apps, YouTube explanations, practice tests, or grammar workbooks.

**Current gap:** He is active but not fully guided. He does not know if he is practicing the right skills or moving fast enough.

**Decision pressure:** Every week matters. A vague school offer feels risky because it may waste preparation time.

---

## Current Situation

### Exam Preparation Reality

**The daily struggle:**

- He studies in fragments and worries that the plan is incomplete.
- He sees many resources but cannot easily tell which ones matter for his exact exam.
- He wants feedback, not just more tasks.
- He needs a realistic path from current level to target score.

**Tools and behavior:**

- Saves exam-prep posts and school pages.
- Compares tutors, schools, and language platforms.
- Looks for exam names, teacher credibility, prices, and start speed.
- Messages providers when the page names his exact problem clearly.

**The preparation gap:**

He does not need "English for everyone." He needs proof that Fluyo understands his exam, his timeline, and the difference between general English and score-focused preparation.

---

## Psychological Profile

### Personality & Motivations

**Core Identity:**

- Danylo wants to be prepared and taken seriously.
- He responds to structure, proof, and specific next steps.
- He is skeptical of soft promises when the stakes are measurable.
- He wants the confidence that comes from knowing what to do next.

**Decision style:**

Danylo scans first and reads second. If the page names his exam and shows credible structure, he slows down. If it only says "learn English in a friendly atmosphere," he assumes Fluyo may be better for casual learners than exam preparation.

---

## Internal State

When Danylo lands on the page, he feels **focused**, **pressured**, and **skeptical**. He wants to believe there is a clear path, but he is guarding against wasting time. He is most likely to act when Fluyo reduces uncertainty quickly.

## Usage Context

**Access / Discovery:** Search, Instagram, referral, or a shared school link.

**Emotional State:** He is alert and deadline-aware. He may feel behind even if he has been studying.

**Behavior Pattern:** He scans for exam names, teacher proof, student results, lesson structure, price, and trial details.

**Decision Criteria:** Exact exam fit, credible teachers, visible results, clear trial value, and fast Telegram path.

**Success Outcome:** He messages Fluyo with his exam name, deadline, current level, and interest in a paid trial.

## Driving Forces

### Top 3 Positive Drivers

**1. See a clear path from current level to target score before committing**

- Danylo wants to understand the gap and the plan before he invests.
- Success looks like leaving the trial with a realistic preparation direction.
- **Fluyo Promise:** The trial clarifies exam goal, current level, weak areas, and recommended preparation format.

**2. Feel guided by a teacher who understands his exact exam**

- He needs confidence that the teacher understands exam tasks, timing, and scoring.
- Success looks like hearing language that matches his real exam context.
- **Fluyo Promise:** Exam-specific copy, credentials, and results show that Fluyo handles serious preparation.

**3. Replace scattered self-study with an organized weekly rhythm**

- He wants relief from deciding what to study next.
- Success looks like a repeatable lesson-and-practice rhythm.
- **Fluyo Promise:** Teacher-led lessons, feedback, and next steps create a structured preparation path.

---

### Top 3 Negative Drivers

**1. Fear wasting weeks on lessons that are too general**

- Time is the resource he cannot get back.
- Failure looks like paying for pleasant lessons that do not improve exam readiness.
- **Fluyo Promise:** The page separates exam preparation from general English and shows what makes it specific.

**2. Fear discovering too late that his level is not enough**

- He worries that the gap is bigger than he thinks.
- Failure looks like realizing near the deadline that preparation was unrealistic.
- **Fluyo Promise:** The paid trial acts as a diagnostic first step with a calm level-and-goal conversation.

**3. Frustration with scattered resources and no feedback**

- He is tired of doing tasks without knowing whether they matter.
- Failure looks like activity without progress.
- **Fluyo Promise:** Teacher feedback and structured practice show what to improve next.
