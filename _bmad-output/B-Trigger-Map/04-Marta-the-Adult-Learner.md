# Marta the Adult Learner - Tertiary Persona

> Tertiary persona - the adult who rediscovers English as practical, supportive, and usable

**Priority:** TERTIARY  
**Role in Flywheel:** Broadens Fluyo's appeal through confidence, practical speaking, and natural word of mouth  
**Created:** 2026-06-25  

---

## Profile Summary

Marta understands more English than she speaks. She may have studied for years, but real conversation still feels slow, embarrassing, or blocked. She wants practical lessons that fit her life and help her finally use English without pressure.

She matters because she expresses the emotional heart of the Fluyo promise: English in flow. When the page makes speaking feel achievable and teacher-led without shame, Marta can recognize value organically and take the first step.

## Background

### Learning Journey

**Past experience:** Marta has studied English in school, apps, short courses, or self-study bursts.

**Current level:** She may know grammar and vocabulary passively but struggles to speak fluidly.

**Life context:** Work, family, travel, studies, or personal growth make English useful, but her schedule is busy.

**Pattern:** She restarts with motivation, then stops when lessons feel too abstract, too intense, or too embarrassing.

---

## Current Situation

### Adult Learning Reality

**The daily struggle:**

- She can understand more than she can say.
- She freezes when a real conversation starts.
- She wants correction but not humiliation.
- She wants progress without rebuilding her life around lessons.

**Skills and tools:**

- Uses apps, videos, podcasts, saved posts, and occasional speaking attempts.
- Looks for flexible schedules, supportive teacher tone, practical topics, and clear formats.
- Checks pricing after she feels emotionally safe enough to consider starting.

**The confidence gap:**

Marta does not need another abstract promise of fluency. She needs to feel that English can become usable in her real life, with a teacher who keeps her speaking instead of making her feel small.

---

## Psychological Profile

### Personality & Motivations

**Core Identity:**

- Wants to be capable and independent.
- Feels embarrassed by the gap between knowledge and speech.
- Values practical progress over academic perfection.
- Needs encouragement that still feels adult and respectful.

**Decision style:**

Marta reads tone carefully. She can sense when a school sounds too childish, too corporate, or too pushy. She responds to calm confidence: practical speaking, supportive correction, flexible formats, and a first step that does not feel like a trap.

---

## Internal State

When Marta lands on the page, she feels **hopeful**, **self-conscious**, and **careful**. She wants to believe she can start again, but she is protecting herself from another failed attempt.

---

## Usage Context

**Access / Discovery:** Instagram, referral, search for online speaking lessons, or seeing Fluyo through another learner.

**Emotional State:** Interested but wary. She imagines herself speaking and feels both desire and fear.

**Behavior Pattern:** Reads adult program copy, checks teacher tone, looks for flexible formats, then evaluates price.

**Decision Criteria:** Speaking practice, supportive correction, real-life topics, schedule fit, individual/pair/group options, and no intimidating academic tone.

**Success Outcome:** She messages Telegram believing she can explain her goal and be matched with a teacher who will help her speak more.

---

## Driving Forces

### Top 3 Positive Drivers

**1. Start speaking without feeling judged**

- Marta wants correction that helps her continue, not correction that shuts her down.
- Success looks like speaking more in each lesson, even with mistakes.
- **Fluyo Answer:** Warm teacher tone, supportive correction language, and speaking-focused lessons make the first step emotionally safe.

**2. Use English for real situations, not abstract textbook progress**

- She wants English for travel, work, meetings, studies, relocation, or everyday life.
- Success looks like practicing phrases and situations she can actually use.
- **Fluyo Answer:** Adult program copy names real-life situations and shows practical lesson topics.

**3. Build a rhythm that survives a busy schedule**

- She needs consistency without an unrealistic plan.
- Success looks like a format she can keep after the first burst of motivation.
- **Fluyo Answer:** Flexible lesson formats and clear next steps reduce commitment anxiety.

---

### Top 3 Negative Drivers

**1. Fear freezing during live conversation**

- She may know the words but lose them under pressure.
- Failure looks like silence, panic, and shame.
- **Fluyo Answer:** Lessons are positioned as guided speaking practice with patient correction and realistic conversation tasks.

**2. Fear starting again and failing again**

- Another abandoned attempt would reinforce the belief that English is not for her.
- Failure looks like paying, trying briefly, then stopping.
- **Fluyo Answer:** The page frames the paid trial as a small, personal, achievable first step.

**3. Frustration with lessons that teach knowledge but not usable speech**

- She is tired of grammar knowledge that does not become conversation.
- Failure looks like understanding exercises but freezing in real life.
- **Fluyo Answer:** Speaking focus, practical topics, teacher feedback, and lesson screenshots prove lessons are active.

## How Marta Discovers Fluyo Value

### The Recognition Path

**Phase 1: Experience the Difference**

She sees copy that names her exact feeling: knowing English but not speaking freely.
