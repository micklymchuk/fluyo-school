## Source Context

The Phase 1 brief confirms three launch paths:

1. Exam preparation
2. Parents/kids
3. Adults

This target-group draft keeps those three as the focus. Teens and Business English remain secondary program signals, but they should not compete with these three launch paths in first-screen strategy.

## Prioritized Target Groups

### 1. Danylo the Deadline-Driven Exam Student - Primary Conversion Persona

**Group:** Exam preparation students  
**Priority:** Primary  
**Role in business goals:** High-intent trial bookings, proof-sensitive conversion, result-oriented testimonials  

**Persona summary:** Danylo is preparing for a high-stakes English exam: NMT, EVI, Cambridge, TOEFL, CELPIP, or a similar goal. He may be a senior school student, university applicant, master's applicant, or early-career professional. His need is specific, time-bound, and measurable: he wants to know whether Fluyo can help him prepare seriously without wasting precious weeks.

**Narrative:**

Danylo is not browsing English schools for inspiration. He is comparing options because there is a deadline on the calendar and a score he needs to reach. He has probably tried self-study, free resources, YouTube explanations, or grammar drills. Some helped, but he still does not feel fully guided. He wants a teacher who can look at his current level, identify weak spots, and build a realistic path.

He is practical but anxious. He looks for proof quickly: exam names, teacher experience, result screenshots, certificates, sample materials, and clear lesson structure. A beautiful brand helps only if it signals discipline and credibility. If the page feels vague, he will assume the school is more lifestyle-oriented than exam-serious.

**Usage context:**

- **Discovery:** Search, Instagram, referral, or a parent/friend sharing the school.
- **Device/context:** Often mobile, between study sessions, after seeing a deadline or realizing self-study is not enough.
- **Emotional state:** Focused, pressured, skeptical of generic promises.
- **Behavior pattern:** Scans for his exact exam first, then checks teachers, format, price, and what happens in the trial.
- **Decision criteria:** Specific exam coverage, visible structure, credible teachers, real student results, ability to start quickly.
- **Success outcome:** He clicks Telegram already knowing which exam he needs, his deadline, and that the trial will clarify a preparation plan.

**Relationship to business goals:**

- **Turn Qualified Interest Into Paid Trial Bookings:** Danylo has clear urgency and a concrete reason to book now.
- **Build Trust Before The Visitor Compares Price:** He needs proof that Fluyo handles serious exam preparation, not only casual speaking.
- **Make Each Launch Audience Feel Immediately Recognized:** The exam path must name exams, show process, and answer result-risk concerns.

**Current behavior:**

Danylo compares schools, tutors, and platforms. He may message several providers at once. He saves pages that mention his exact exam and rejects pages that only say "English for all levels." If pricing is hidden or the exam path is vague, he moves on or asks Telegram questions that the page should have answered.

### 2. Olena the Observant Parent - Trust And Retention Persona

**Group:** Parents choosing English lessons for children 6+  
**Priority:** Secondary  
**Role in business goals:** Parent-funded bookings, long-term retention, parent testimonials, word-of-mouth trust  

**Persona summary:** Olena is choosing lessons for her child, not herself. She wants English to feel warm and enjoyable, but she is still the buyer and evaluator. She needs to believe the teacher is safe, patient, competent, and able to keep a child engaged online.

**Narrative:**

Olena has seen many online lesson offers. Some look too childish, some too chaotic, and some too marketplace-like. She is not only asking "Will my child learn English?" She is asking "Will my child like this teacher, will I see progress, and will this be worth adding to our family schedule?"

She notices details. Teacher faces matter. Lesson screenshots matter. Parent testimonials matter. A clear explanation of feedback, progress, and age-appropriate materials matters. She may not need a long methodology essay, but she does need enough evidence to feel that Fluyo understands children and respects parents' need for visibility.

**Usage context:**

- **Discovery:** Instagram, referral from another parent, search for children's online English, or a saved school profile.
- **Device/context:** Mobile during a busy day; sometimes desktop when comparing prices and formats.
- **Emotional state:** Protective, curious, cautious about online quality.
- **Behavior pattern:** Looks at teacher photos, child-appropriate lesson visuals, parent feedback, prices, and FAQs before booking.
- **Decision criteria:** Teacher warmth, child safety, visible progress, flexible schedule, clear parent communication, small-group size.
- **Success outcome:** She clicks Telegram with the child's age, current level, schedule constraints, and confidence that the trial will be gentle and useful.

**Relationship to business goals:**

- **Turn Qualified Interest Into Paid Trial Bookings:** Olena books when the first step feels safe and concrete.
- **Build Trust Before The Visitor Compares Price:** She is the segment most likely to need proof before price feels acceptable.
- **Make Each Launch Audience Feel Immediately Recognized:** The kids path must speak to the parent and show how the child experiences lessons.

**Current behavior:**

Olena asks other parents, compares school Instagram pages, checks whether teachers look real, and reads every detail about the trial. If the page hides practical details, she will message many questions before deciding. If she does not see progress visibility, she may postpone.

### 3. Marta the Momentum-Seeking Adult - Confidence And Flexibility Persona

**Group:** Adults learning English for life, work, travel, studies, or personal growth  
**Priority:** Tertiary  
**Role in business goals:** Broad trial demand, speaking-focused positioning, recurring lessons, practical success stories  

**Persona summary:** Marta understands more English than she speaks. She may have studied for years, but real conversation still feels slow, embarrassing, or blocked. She wants practical lessons that fit her life and help her finally use English without pressure.

**Narrative:**

Marta is tired of starting over. She has work, errands, family, or studies, so she cannot commit to a complicated system. She wants a format that respects her schedule and a teacher who will correct her without making her feel small. The promise of "flow" matters deeply here: she wants English to become lighter, more natural, and less loaded with old school anxiety.

She does not need a hard deadline like Danylo, so her conversion depends on emotional recognition and low-friction clarity. She wants to see practical speaking, real-life topics, flexible formats, and a paid trial that feels like a manageable first step rather than a sales trap.

**Usage context:**

- **Discovery:** Instagram, recommendation, search for online speaking lessons, or seeing the brand through a friend.
- **Device/context:** Mobile in the evening, during commute, or after a moment where English felt necessary.
- **Emotional state:** Hopeful but self-conscious; interested but wary of repeating old failures.
- **Behavior pattern:** Reads the promise and adult program copy more carefully, checks format flexibility and teacher tone, then looks at pricing.
- **Decision criteria:** Speaking practice, supportive correction, flexible schedule, practical topics, individual/pair/group options, no intimidating academic tone.
- **Success outcome:** She clicks Telegram believing she can start gently, explain her goal, and be matched with a teacher who will help her speak more.

**Relationship to business goals:**

- **Turn Qualified Interest Into Paid Trial Bookings:** Marta converts when the first step feels easy, personal, and non-intimidating.
- **Build Trust Before The Visitor Compares Price:** She needs proof that the school is supportive and practical, not just polished.
- **Make Each Launch Audience Feel Immediately Recognized:** The adult path must name real-life use cases and speak to speaking anxiety directly.

**Current behavior:**

Marta saves English-learning posts, tries apps, watches short videos, and occasionally considers a tutor. She hesitates because she fears paying for lessons and still freezing when speaking. If the page makes English feel achievable and teacher-led without pressure, she is likely to try the paid trial.

## Target Group Prioritization Rationale

| Priority | Persona | Why This Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Danylo the Deadline-Driven Exam Student | Highest urgency and clearest conversion intent; proof and structure can move him quickly to Telegram. |
| 2 | Olena the Observant Parent | Strong trust requirements and likely long-term lesson value; parent reassurance can create durable retention and referrals. |
| 3 | Marta the Momentum-Seeking Adult | Broad audience with strong fit to the "English in flow" promise; conversion depends on confidence, flexibility, and emotional safety. |

## Cross-Group Patterns

### Shared Needs

- Clear first step: what the trial includes, how booking works, and what happens after Telegram contact.
- Real teacher proof: faces, bios, credentials, teaching style, certificates, and experience.
- Practical lesson visibility: screenshots, materials, speaking activities, feedback, and progress cues.
- Direct price clarity before contact.
- Warm but precise tone: confident enough to trust, human enough to reduce anxiety.

### Key Differences

- **Danylo** needs urgency, structure, exam specificity, and result credibility.
- **Olena** needs safety, teacher warmth, parent-visible progress, and child-fit reassurance.
- **Marta** needs emotional permission to start again, flexible formats, and speaking confidence without shame.

### Design Implications To Carry Forward

- The hero can stay broad, but the next interaction must route visitors into the three paths immediately.
- Each path needs its own proof type, not one generic proof block.
- The Telegram CTA should preserve context where possible: exam, child age, adult speaking goal, format preference.
- Pricing should support comparison without making visitors feel pushed into one format too early.

## Research Applied

- Preply emphasizes tutor profiles, reviews, ratings, specialties, pricing, trial booking, and goal/schedule matching.
- Novakid emphasizes parent reassurance: teacher qualifications, child progress tracking, post-lesson feedback, and trial preparation.
- Lingoda emphasizes certified teachers, live classes, CEFR levels, speaking from day one, structured curriculum, feedback, and flexible class formats.
