# Driving Forces

> Positive and negative motivation psychology for Fluyo School's core personas

**Document:** Trigger Map - Driving Forces  
**Created:** 2026-06-25  
**Status:** Approved  
**Mode:** Suggest  

---

## Framework

Driving forces describe what pulls each persona toward Fluyo and what pushes them away from their current situation.

- **Positive drivers:** What they want to achieve, gain, experience, or become.
- **Negative drivers:** What they want to avoid, escape, reduce, or prevent.

Negative drivers are especially important because loss, embarrassment, wasted time, and uncertainty often create stronger action pressure than positive aspiration alone.

## 1. Danylo the Deadline-Driven Exam Student

**Context:** Danylo is preparing for a high-stakes English exam and wants a serious, structured path before time runs out.

### Positive Drivers

**1. See a clear path from current level to target score before committing**

Danylo wants to know that the school can assess where he is now, identify the score gap, and explain how lessons will move him toward the result. This matters when he is comparing options and does not want another vague "we will improve your English" promise.

**Fluyo Promise:** The trial lesson clarifies his exam goal, current level, weak areas, and recommended preparation format.

**2. Feel guided by a teacher who understands his exact exam**

He wants confidence that the teacher knows the exam structure, common mistakes, timing pressure, and scoring expectations. He needs an expert guide, not only a friendly conversation partner.

**Fluyo Promise:** Exam-specific program copy, teacher credentials, and results proof show that Fluyo can handle serious preparation.

**3. Replace scattered self-study with an organized weekly rhythm**

Danylo wants the relief of knowing what to practice next, what matters most, and how to use limited time. Structure gives him momentum and reduces decision fatigue.

**Fluyo Promise:** Lessons combine teacher feedback, practice tasks, homework direction, and measurable progress checkpoints.

**4. Prove to himself and others that he is making progress**

Exam preparation has social pressure: parents, university plans, career plans, or self-expectations. Danylo wants visible progress he can point to before the exam date.

**Fluyo Promise:** Progress language, student results, and lesson feedback make improvement visible instead of abstract.

### Negative Drivers

**1. Fear wasting weeks on lessons that are too general**

Danylo is afraid of paying for "English lessons" that do not directly improve his exam readiness. Time is his scarcest resource.

**Fluyo Answer:** The page must name exam paths clearly and show what makes exam preparation different from general English.

**2. Fear discovering too late that his level is not enough**

He worries that he is underprepared and will only understand the size of the gap near the deadline. This creates urgency and anxiety.

**Fluyo Answer:** The trial should feel diagnostic: a fast, calm way to understand level, deadline, and preparation plan.

**3. Fear looking unprepared or disappointing others**

Poor exam performance can feel public: family expectations, university admission, job requirements, or personal pride. He wants to avoid shame as much as he wants a high score.

**Fluyo Answer:** Messaging should frame preparation as focused and realistic, not judgmental.

**4. Frustration with scattered resources and no feedback**

YouTube videos, worksheets, and apps create activity but not always direction. He is tired of practicing without knowing whether he is improving the right skills.

**Fluyo Answer:** Teacher-led feedback, structured practice, and clear next steps should be visible before the CTA.

## 2. Olena the Observant Parent

**Context:** Olena is deciding whether Fluyo is safe, warm, and effective enough for her child.

### Positive Drivers

**1. Feel that her child will be emotionally safe with the teacher**

Olena wants to see patience, warmth, and child-friendly professionalism. Her child may be shy, energetic, easily distracted, or nervous about online lessons.

**Fluyo Promise:** Teacher portraits, short bios, teaching style notes, and parent testimonials make the people behind the school feel real.

**2. See that online lessons can keep a child engaged**

She wants proof that lessons are not just a video call with a textbook. Interactive tasks, visuals, games, speaking practice, and age-appropriate materials help her believe the format can work.

**Fluyo Promise:** Lesson screenshots and a "how lessons work for kids" explanation show the learning experience.

**3. Understand how progress will be visible to parents**

Olena wants to know what her child learned, whether the child participates, and what happens next. Progress visibility makes the purchase feel responsible.

**Fluyo Promise:** Parent feedback, teacher updates, level/path language, and progress examples reduce uncertainty.

**4. Find a format that fits family schedule and budget**

She wants an option that works around school, activities, family routines, and price sensitivity without sacrificing quality.

**Fluyo Promise:** Direct pricing and format comparison make individual, pair, and mini-group options easy to evaluate.

### Negative Drivers

**1. Fear paying for lessons her child does not enjoy**

If the child resists lessons, the family loses money, time, and emotional energy. Olena wants to avoid another activity that becomes a weekly battle.

**Fluyo Answer:** The trial should feel like a gentle fit check for the child, not only a sales step for the parent.

**2. Fear that online lessons are less serious or less effective**

Some parents still suspect online learning is passive, chaotic, or easy to ignore. She needs proof that the online format is structured and interactive.

**Fluyo Answer:** Show real lesson materials, online classroom flow, teacher feedback, and child participation cues.

**3. Fear not knowing what is happening inside the lesson**

Because she is not the learner, Olena needs visibility. Without updates, she may feel disconnected from the value she is paying for.

**Fluyo Answer:** Explain how parents receive feedback, understand progress, and communicate with the teacher or manager.

**4. Fear choosing the wrong teacher or level**

She worries that the child will be mismatched, bored, overwhelmed, or uncomfortable.

**Fluyo Answer:** The trial and placement process should explain how Fluyo chooses the right teacher, level, and format.

## 3. Marta the Momentum-Seeking Adult

**Context:** Marta wants practical speaking confidence but carries old frustration from years of studying without speaking freely.

### Positive Drivers

**1. Start speaking without feeling judged**

Marta wants a teacher who corrects her gently and helps her keep talking. She wants progress without humiliation.

**Fluyo Promise:** Warm teacher tone, supportive correction language, and speaking-focused lessons make the first step feel emotionally safe.

**2. Use English for real situations, not abstract textbook progress**

She wants English for travel, meetings, work conversations, interviews, relocation, studies, or everyday confidence. Practical relevance keeps her motivated.

**Fluyo Promise:** Adult program copy should name real-life situations and show lesson topics.

**3. Build a rhythm that survives a busy schedule**

Marta needs lessons that fit around work, family, and energy. She wants consistency without feeling trapped by an unrealistic plan.

**Fluyo Promise:** Flexible formats, clear lesson length, and manageable next steps reduce commitment anxiety.

**4. Feel the emotional shift from "I know English but cannot speak" to "I can handle this"**

Her real goal is confidence and identity, not just vocabulary. She wants English to feel lighter and more available in the moment.

**Fluyo Promise:** The "English in flow" positioning directly supports this transformation when backed by practical proof.

### Negative Drivers

**1. Fear freezing during live conversation**

Marta may understand English passively but panic when she has to respond. She fears silence, mistakes, and losing face.

**Fluyo Answer:** Position lessons as guided speaking practice with patient correction and realistic conversation tasks.

**2. Fear starting again and failing again**

She may have tried apps, courses, school lessons, or tutors before. Another failed restart would reinforce the belief that English is not for her.

**Fluyo Answer:** The page should make the first step small, personal, and achievable instead of promising instant fluency.

**3. Fear being placed in a group where she feels behind**

Groups can feel efficient but socially risky. She worries about comparison, speed, and embarrassment.

**Fluyo Answer:** Explain levels, format options, and how matching works so group learning feels safe rather than exposed.

**4. Frustration with lessons that teach knowledge but not usable speech**

She is tired of knowing grammar rules but not being able to speak naturally when it matters.

**Fluyo Answer:** Speaking focus, practical topics, teacher feedback, and lesson screenshots must prove lessons are active.

## Cross-Group Patterns

### Shared Drivers

**1. Reassurance before contact**

All three personas need enough clarity before Telegram: what happens in the trial, who teaches, how the format works, and what the next step looks like.

**2. Teacher trust is the emotional center**

The brand can be premium and elegant, but conversion depends on believable teachers: real faces, credentials, style, warmth, and fit.

**3. Visible progress reduces perceived risk**

Danylo wants exam progress, Olena wants child progress, and Marta wants speaking progress. Each needs a different proof language, but the underlying driver is the same.

**4. Direct pricing supports trust**

Hidden prices would increase anxiety for all groups. Direct format comparison helps visitors decide whether contacting Fluyo is worth the next step.

### Unique Drivers

- **Danylo:** Deadline pressure, target score anxiety, exam-specific credibility.
- **Olena:** Child safety, teacher warmth, parent-visible progress, family schedule fit.
- **Marta:** Speaking shame, restart fatigue, need for low-pressure confidence.

### Potential Tensions

**1. Exam seriousness vs. brand warmth**

Danylo needs rigor, while Marta and Olena need emotional comfort. The page must show Fluyo is warm without looking unserious.

**2. Parent reassurance vs. adult autonomy**

Parents need visibility and communication; adults need independence and non-patronizing tone. Copy and sections should be segmented clearly.

**3. Direct pricing vs. personalized path**

Pricing must be transparent, but the final recommendation may depend on goal, level, format, and teacher fit. The page should show prices while making the trial useful for choosing the right option.

---

## Design Signals To Carry Forward

- Use path-specific proof: exam results for Danylo, parent feedback and lesson screenshots for Olena, speaking transformation and practical topics for Marta.
- Make the trial lesson function clear: fit check, goal/level conversation, teacher recommendation, and next-step plan.
- Keep Telegram CTA context-aware: exam goal, child age, adult speaking goal, format preference.
- Avoid generic claims like "improve your English" unless paired with concrete usage context.
- Preserve the "Fluent + Flow" promise, but ground it in teacher-led structure and visible progress.

## Research Applied

- Language anxiety research identifies communication apprehension, test anxiety, and fear of negative evaluation as major forces in language learning.
- Test anxiety literature reinforces why deadline-driven students need structure, diagnostic clarity, and reduced uncertainty.
- Parent-facing online education patterns emphasize teacher qualification, child engagement, progress visibility, and parent-teacher communication.
- Comparable language-learning pages from Preply, Novakid, and Lingoda foreground teacher proof, structured paths, trial/first-step clarity, progress, and flexible formats.
