## Prioritization Method

This document combines two prioritization lenses:

1. **Strategic ranking with challenged reasoning:** every priority includes why it ranks above the next alternative.
2. **Driver scoring:** each driver is scored from 1-5 for Frequency, Intensity, and Product Fit.

**Driver score = Frequency + Intensity + Fit**

| Score | Priority | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 14-15 | Must Address | Core to conversion or trust; design should fail review if this is missing. |
| 11-13 | Should Address | Important, but can be satisfied through supporting content or later refinement. |
| 8-10 | Could Address | Useful, but not central to the first launch page. |
| <8 | Defer | Not a strong fit for this landing page. |

Tie-break rule: when a positive and negative driver score equally, the negative driver wins because avoiding loss, embarrassment, wasted money, or uncertainty often creates stronger action pressure.

## 1. Business Goal Priority

### Priority 1: Turn Qualified Interest Into Paid Trial Bookings

**Why this outranks trust and audience recognition:** Trial booking is the commercial engine. Trust and segmentation matter because they increase booking quality and conversion, but they are enablers rather than the final business outcome.

**Challenge considered:** Could "Build Trust Before Price" be first?  
**Decision:** No. Trust is essential, but if the page builds trust without moving visitors to Telegram and paid trial booking, the business goal is not achieved.

### Priority 2: Build Trust Before The Visitor Compares Price

**Why this outranks audience recognition:** A visitor can recognize their path and still refuse to book if the school feels unproven. For a paid trial lesson, proof must come before price sensitivity becomes the deciding factor.

**Challenge considered:** Could audience recognition be second?  
**Decision:** Not for this launch. Recognition helps visitors navigate, but teacher proof, results, testimonials, and lesson visibility carry the risk reduction needed for paid action.

### Priority 3: Make Each Launch Audience Feel Immediately Recognized

**Why this remains important:** Fluyo serves multiple segments. Without immediate routing into exams, kids, and adults, the page risks sounding generic and forcing visitors to work too hard to find themselves.

**Challenge considered:** Is this just content organization?  
**Decision:** No. It is a conversion requirement, but it serves the first two goals rather than outranking them.

## 2. Top-Goal Objective Priority

Top goal: **Turn Qualified Interest Into Paid Trial Bookings**

### Priority 1: Paid Trial Booking Conversion

**Metric:** Telegram trial inquiries that become paid trial bookings.  
**Target:** 35-50% conversion from qualified Telegram inquiries in the first 90 days.

**Why this outranks CTA clicks:** Clicks are not enough. If visitors click Telegram but do not book the paid trial, the page may be generating curiosity rather than commitment.

### Priority 2: Qualified Inquiry Quality

**Metric:** Share of Telegram inquiries that mention program path, learner goal, age/level, or preferred lesson format.  
**Target:** 70%+ of trial inquiries include at least one qualifying detail in the first 60 days.

**Why this outranks raw CTA engagement:** A lower volume of well-qualified inquiries can be more valuable than high-volume vague messages that add manager workload and do not convert.

### Priority 3: Trial CTA Engagement

**Metric:** Visitor-to-Telegram CTA click rate.  
**Target:** 8-12% of qualified landing-page visitors in the first 90 days.

**Why this is third, not first:** CTA engagement is necessary, but it is upstream. It should be optimized only in ways that preserve booking intent and inquiry quality.

## 3. Target Group Priority

### Priority 1: Danylo the Deadline-Driven Exam Student

**Why Danylo outranks Olena and Marta:** He has the clearest urgency, the sharpest goal, and the strongest reason to act now. Exam names, deadlines, teacher proof, and results can move him quickly from page to Telegram.

**Tradeoff accepted:** This gives the first launch page a slightly more serious conversion spine. The design still needs warmth, but not at the expense of exam credibility.

### Priority 2: Olena the Observant Parent

**Why Olena outranks Marta:** She is both decision maker and payer, and her trust barrier is high. If Fluyo earns parent confidence, the relationship can produce retention, testimonials, and referrals.

**Tradeoff accepted:** Parent content needs more reassurance and proof, which costs page space. It earns that space because it reduces paid-trial risk.

### Priority 3: Marta the Momentum-Seeking Adult

**Why Marta is third:** She fits the brand promise very well, but her urgency is usually softer than exam-prep demand and her decision may take more emotional warming.

**Tradeoff accepted:** Adult learning should remain visible and persuasive, but it should not dilute the first-screen focus or proof hierarchy.

## 5. MoSCoW Design Focus

### Must Address

1. **Paid trial clarity**
   - What the trial includes.
   - How Telegram booking works.
   - What happens after the visitor messages.
   - Why a paid trial has value.

2. **Exam-specific credibility**
   - Named exams: NMT, EVI, Cambridge, TOEFL, CELPIP.
   - Teacher expertise and preparation structure.
   - Results, certificates, or proof specific to exam preparation.

3. **Teacher trust**
   - Real teacher portraits.
   - Credentials and short bios.
   - Teaching style and warmth.
   - Proof that teachers can support children, adults, and exam students.

4. **Path-specific proof**
   - Exam path: results, deadlines, structure, diagnostic plan.
   - Kids path: child engagement, parent reassurance, progress visibility.
   - Adults path: speaking practice, practical topics, supportive correction.

5. **Transparent pricing and formats**
   - Paid trial price and duration once confirmed.
   - Individual, pair, and mini-group pricing.
   - Mini-group cap of four students.
   - Clear comparison without hiding details behind contact.

### Should Address

1. Parent feedback and progress communication.
2. Lesson screenshots and material examples.
3. Adult real-life topic examples.
4. Level and format matching explanation.
5. Bilingual UA/EN copy quality across all conversion-critical sections.
6. Context-aware Telegram messages or CTA labels by audience path.

### Could Address

1. Business English as a secondary adult sub-path.
2. Teens as a secondary program mention.
3. More detailed teacher galleries.
4. Extended FAQ for payment, rescheduling, levels, and materials.
5. Future SEO content for separate program pages.

### Not For First Launch Focus

1. Marketplace-style tutor browsing.
2. A complex placement quiz before Telegram.
3. Hidden pricing or "contact for price" as the primary pricing strategy.
4. Making Business English or Teens equal first-screen paths unless strategy changes.

## 6. Design Focus Statement

Design the Fluyo School landing page primarily for **Danylo the Deadline-Driven Exam Student**, while strongly supporting **Olena the Observant Parent** and keeping **Marta the Momentum-Seeking Adult** visible as the broad confidence path.

The page must transform visitors from "I am interested, but I need proof and clarity before I pay for a trial" into "I understand my path, I trust the teachers, I know what the trial does, and I am ready to message Fluyo on Telegram."

**Primary design target:** Danylo, because urgent exam preparation creates the clearest launch conversion opportunity.

**Secondary design target:** Olena, because parent trust can create retention and referral value.

**Supporting design target:** Marta, because adult speaking confidence expresses the brand promise and broadens demand.

---

## 7. Strategic Tradeoffs

### What gets more attention

- Exam preparation proof and structure.
- Teacher credibility.
- Trial lesson clarity.
- Path-specific reassurance.
- Pricing transparency.

### What gets less attention for now

- Full marketplace-style teacher selection.
- Deep Business English positioning.
- Teens as a separate primary journey.
- Long methodology explanations.
- Decorative brand elements that do not support trust or conversion.

### Why this is the right tradeoff

The launch page has to convert paid trial bookings, not simply explain the entire school. The strongest path is clear: route visitors quickly, prove teacher-led trust, show the trial's value, and make Telegram the obvious next step.

## Research Applied

- MoSCoW prioritization supports separating Must, Should, Could, and out-of-scope items so launch focus is explicit.
- Loss-aversion theory supports giving negative drivers extra attention when they represent wasted time, embarrassment, failed fit, or uncertainty.
- Prior comparable scan reinforced that language-learning pages convert through teacher proof, trial clarity, progress proof, pricing/format clarity, and audience-specific paths.
