# Addendum: Fluyo School Landing Page

## Source Vision Extract

Fluyo School is a modern online English school. The brand meaning is "Fluent + Flow": English learning should feel natural, confident, and manageable rather than overwhelming.

The school teaches practical, real-life English through teacher-led lessons, interactive activities, speaking practice, and vocabulary students can use in everyday life, work, travel, studies, and exams.

The page should make visitors think: "This is exactly the type of school I have been looking for."

## Audience Detail

Current audience list:

- Children 6+
- Teenagers
- Adults
- A1-C1 learners
- NMT, EVI, Cambridge, TOEFL, and CELPIP exam-prep students
- Business English learners
- People who want to start speaking confidently

Confirmed primary launch paths:

- Exam preparation
- Parents/kids
- Adults

Design implication: the page should use a broad brand promise in the hero, then quickly segment into these three paths. Teens and business English can appear as secondary programs, but should not compete with the three launch paths in the first screen.

## Visual Reference Interpretation

Provided references include:

- Current Fluyo School logo: white base, burgundy script wordmark, circular frame.
- Instagram profile: current public positioning includes English A0-C1, kids, teens, adults, individual, pairs, mini-groups, NMT, EVI, Cambridge, TOEFL, and Telegram manager contact.
- Cookie, coffee, candle, globe, and books: tactile, warm, cozy, premium, editorial object photography.
- Wax seals and stamp-style icons: strong candidate for signature icon system and section markers.
- Black wax icon disks with graduation cap and chat bubble: useful direction for program/communication feature icons.
- Price-tag wax stamp: possible pricing section visual metaphor.
- Globe and books: classic learning/worldliness references, best used sparingly so the brand does not become generic.

Design direction:

- White and light backgrounds with deep burgundy and charcoal contrast.
- Large whitespace, clean typography, refined motion.
- Rounded forms are acceptable, but the page should feel elegant, not childish.
- 3D assets should feel custom and tactile rather than plastic or generic.
- Wax-stamp motifs can become a signature system for programs, benefits, pricing, and contact points.

Avoid:

- Busy layouts
- Rainbow education palette
- Cartoon-style illustrations
- Generic stock photos
- Dense screens with too much text
- Corporate SaaS coldness

## Candidate Page Structure

1. Hero: Fluyo promise, short explanation, primary CTA.
2. Program paths: exam preparation, parents/kids, adults.
3. Why Fluyo: speaking focus, personalized path, small groups, friendly teachers, modern materials.
4. How lessons work: level check, format choice, online lesson, progress.
5. Teachers/team: real portraits and credibility.
6. Lesson experience: screenshots or clean visual explanation of Zoom/Miro/digital materials.
7. Pricing and formats: direct prices for trial lesson, individual lessons, pairs, and mini-groups.
8. Testimonials/results/proof.
9. FAQ.
10. Final CTA and Telegram contact.

## Light Comparable Scan

Sources checked on 2026-06-25:

- Preply English tutors: https://preply.com/en/online/english-tutors
- Cambly English: https://www.cambly.com/english?lang=en
- Novakid: https://www.novakidschool.com/

Observed patterns:

- Preply leans heavily on choice and proof: tutor count, ratings, reviews, prices, specialties, trial lesson, tutor replacement, and a "how it works" sequence.
- Cambly leads with real-life speaking and progress, then frames the experience around goals, tutors, progress tracking, testimonials, and plan options.
- Novakid uses a kid-specific funnel: age/name quiz, parent trust, lesson mechanics, teacher qualifications, pricing, free trial, and detailed FAQ around the trial lesson.

Implications for Fluyo:

- Premium visuals alone will not convert. The page needs proof: teachers, reviews, lesson screenshots, format clarity, and trial details.
- Fluyo can avoid looking like a marketplace by presenting a curated school experience, not an endless tutor catalog.
- The page should make the first step extremely clear: what happens after booking, who contacts the student, what the trial includes, and how the right teacher/program is chosen.
- If parents are a primary audience, the page needs parent-specific reassurance: safety, teacher quality, lesson feedback, progress visibility, and age-appropriate materials.
- If adults/professionals are primary, the page needs outcome language around speaking confidence, work, travel, interviews, meetings, and schedule flexibility.
